Reflection: 7 Cures for a Lean Purse

Chapter: The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

Arkad, once a poor scribe and now the richest man in Babylon, is asked to teach others how he got there. He gives seven "cures," which read as a sequence of habits:

  1. Start thy purse to fattening. Keep at least a tenth of everything you earn. The point is the habit of keeping, not the size.
  2. Control thy expenditures. Wants expand to fill income, so separate real necessities from desires and budget accordingly.
  3. Make thy gold multiply. Savings sitting idle do nothing. Put them to work so they earn.
  4. Guard thy treasures from loss. Seek safety and the counsel of people experienced with money before chasing high returns.
  5. Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment. Owning your home beats paying rent forever.
  6. Insure a future income. Provide for old age and for your family before you need to.
  7. Increase thy ability to earn. Skill, learning, and desire are the source of everything else.

The order matters. Cures one and two are about behavior, three and four about protecting and growing money, five and six about security, and seven about becoming more valuable. Arkad's central insight is that a lean purse comes from habits, not from bad luck or a low wage. It also has to be a full purse, since wealth comes from what you keep, not from what you earn.

Reflection: 5 Laws of Gold

Chapter: The Five Laws of Gold

The Five Laws of Gold In this chapter, the setting changes. Old Kalabab narrates to his caravan team the story of his son, Nomasir, who gets sent by Arkad, his father, for ten years, accompanied by a bag of gold and a clay tablet t hat bears the five laws engraved on it. He fails to pay attention to the tablet initially and ends up losing everything, including betting on a horse race rigged by some friends. After losing everything, he begins reading the laws, saving, and investing through cautious and experienced people. On his return, he hands over the original bag of gold and an additional two bags to show the value of the wisdom gained. The laws themselves are as follows:

  • Gold goes to people who save at least one tenth of their earnings.
  • It serves its owner when he uses it.
  • It remains with cautious owners who seek expert counsel.
  • It runs away from people who invest in unfamiliar things.
  • It runs away from people chasing impossible returns or listening to schemers.
Theme of the chapter is that wisdom comes before gold as untrained hands lose money fast.
